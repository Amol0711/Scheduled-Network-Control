Reading and execution guide

This companion specifies the numerical models, experiments, estimators and recorded results in this snapshot. It supplies complete readable records alongside the executable code and machine-readable data. The mathematical arguments establishing control guarantees remain in the journal documents. Computational checks and numerical evaluation of certificate constants do not replace those arguments.

The original scientific kernels and recorded data are preserved. Tables C1–C4 reproduce the journal-version numerical tables without changing their cells, units or uncertainty interpretation. Their former identifiers S1–S4 are mapped in Record locations. Numerical equations below have local identifiers E01–E10, independent of journal numbering. C01–C09 are stable section identifiers. The evidence index maps each figure panel and table to its methods, data and code.

C01. Models, metrics and distinct experiment families

Deterministic network and metrics

There are N=6N=6 planar nodes. Let J=(0−110)J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right) and

f(x)=αx+ωJx−bSL∥x∥22x,α=bSL=1,ω=2. f(x)=\alpha x+\omega Jx-b_{\mathrm{SL}}\|x\|_2^2x, \qquad \alpha=b_{\mathrm{SL}}=1,\quad \omega=2.

Let X=col(x1,…,xN)X=\operatorname{col}(x_1,\ldots,x_N), F(X)=col(f(x1),…,f(xN))F(X)=\operatorname{col}(f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_N)), and let LL be the unit-weight undirected graph Laplacian. Define the arithmetic mean y=N−1∑ixiy=N^{-1}\sum_i x_i and the stacked disagreement η=X−𝟏⊗y\eta=X-\mathbf1\otimes y. The network horizon is T=2T=2. Except where a graph-specific gain is specified in C04, the coupling multiplier is k0=1.2k_0=1.2 and the graph is the six-node ring. The reduced reference y‾\bar y solves y‾̇=f(y‾)\dot{\bar y}=f(\bar y) from the arithmetic initial mean. The snapshot is t⋆=T−10−5=1.99999t_\star=T-10^{-5}=1.99999.

The fiber-only deterministic model is

Ẋ=F(X)−k0κ(t)(L⊗I2)X. \dot X=F(X)-k_0\kappa(t)(L\otimes I_2)X. \tag{E01}

Reported disagreement is ∥η∥2\|\eta\|_2, not normalized by NN. Projection-orbit error is ey(t)=∥y(t)−y‾(t)∥2e_y(t)=\|y(t)-\bar y(t)\|_2. Phase error is the continuously tracked angular difference of the mean relative to the reduced reference, in radians. Coupling energy is Ju=∫0t⋆∥−k0κ(L⊗I2)X∥22dtJ_u=\int_0^{t_\star}\|-k_0\kappa(L\otimes I_2)X\|_2^2dt. The full-clock diagnostic requires additional physical input, accounted for in C03. Time and state scales follow the numerical benchmark; no physical-unit conversion is specified in the source records.

The finite-amplitude Cartesian array is defined, in node order, by

xi0=ri(cosθi,sinθi)⊤, x_i^0=r_i(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)^\top, r=(0.75,1.25,0.90,1.15,0.80,1.20),θ=(−0.75,−0.42,−0.15,0.18,0.48,0.82). r=(0.75,1.25,0.90,1.15,0.80,1.20),\qquad \theta=(-0.75,-0.42,-0.15,0.18,0.48,0.82).

Nonlinear stochastic model

Let W=(W1,…,WN)⊤W=(W_1,\ldots,W_N)^\top consist of independent standard node Brownian motions and let eθ(t)=(−sinωt,cosωt)⊤e_\theta(t)=(-\sin\omega t,\cos\omega t)^\top. The implemented equation is

dX=[F(X)−k0κK(t)(L⊗I2)X]dt−k0σκK(t)(L⊗eθ(t))dWt,κK(t)=min{(T−t)−1,K}. \begin{aligned} dX={}&[F(X)-k_0\kappa_K(t)(L\otimes I_2)X]dt\\ &-k_0\sigma\kappa_K(t)(L\otimes e_\theta(t))dW_t, \qquad \kappa_K(t)=\min\{(T-t)^{-1},K\}. \end{aligned}\tag{E02}

The Laplacian acts on node measurement channels, not independent edge channels. The reference tangent is common and deterministic, not a tangent computed from each node or the current mean. Its time-dependent, state-independent diffusion has zero Itô–Stratonovich correction. The numerical rule is left-point Euler–Maruyama. Although 𝟏⊤L=0\mathbf1^\top L=0, nonlinear drift can transmit disagreement effects to the mean.

The nonlinear initial state has unit radii and centered phases

xi(0)=(cosϑi,sinϑi)⊤,ϑi=2(ai−a‾),a=(0.08,−0.05,0.12,−0.10,0.03,−0.08)⊤,a‾=N−1∑iai. \begin{gathered} x_i(0)=(\cos\vartheta_i,\sin\vartheta_i)^\top,\qquad \vartheta_i=2(a_i-\bar a),\\ a=(0.08,-0.05,0.12,-0.10,0.03,-0.08)^\top,\qquad \bar a=N^{-1}\sum_i a_i. \end{gathered}

For a unit eigenvector vjv_j of LL, the scalar comparison starts from ξj0=vj⊤(ϑ−ϑ‾𝟏)\xi_{j0}=v_j^\top(\vartheta-\bar\vartheta\mathbf1). These are phase offsets (arc lengths on the specified unit cycle), not Cartesian sine projections. In co-rotating radial/tangent coordinates, the node variational rates are −2α-2\alpha and zero. Nonzero graph modes are tangent to the node cycle and normal to synchronization. Their scalar comparison is

dξj=−ρjκK(t)ξjdt−ρjσκK(t)dW̃j(t),ρj=k0λj(L). d\xi_j=-\rho_j\kappa_K(t)\xi_jdt-\rho_j\sigma\kappa_K(t)d\widetilde W_j(t), \qquad \rho_j=k_0\lambda_j(L).

Here W̃j\widetilde W_j is the orthogonally transformed Brownian coordinate. This is a variational model, not an exact nonlinear stochastic reduction. The nonlinear terminal cost is squared Euclidean disagreement, ∑i∥xi(T)−y(T)∥22\sum_i\|x_i(T)-y(T)\|_2^2, rather than phase variance. The scalar/modal comparator is evaluated with its declared phase-offset initialization.

Native-plant input and total energy

uc=−k0κ1(L⊗I2)X,uw=(κ1−1)F(X),u=uc+uw. u_c=-k_0\kappa_1(L\otimes I_2)X,\qquad u_w=(\kappa_1-1)F(X),\qquad u=u_c+u_w.
