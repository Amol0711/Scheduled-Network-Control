\section{Reading and execution guide}\label{guide}

This companion specifies the numerical models, experiments, estimators
and recorded results in this snapshot. It supplies complete readable
records alongside the executable code and machine-readable data. The
mathematical arguments establishing control guarantees remain in the
journal documents. Computational checks and numerical evaluation of
certificate constants do not replace those arguments.

The original scientific kernels and recorded data are preserved. Tables
C1--C4 reproduce the journal-version numerical tables without changing
their cells, units or uncertainty interpretation. Their former
identifiers S1--S4 are mapped in \href{RECORD_LOCATIONS.md}{Record
locations}. Numerical equations below have local identifiers E01--E10,
independent of journal numbering. C01--C09 are stable section
identifiers. The \href{EVIDENCE_INDEX.md}{evidence index} maps each
figure panel and table to its methods, data and code.

Read this document as \href{COMPUTATIONAL_COMPANION.pdf}{PDF}, as the
\href{COMPUTATIONAL_COMPANION.html}{offline HTML edition}, or as
Markdown. All document and data links are relative to the snapshot. No
external service, authentication or network connection is needed to read
the supplied PDF or HTML. No hosted address has been assigned to this
local candidate.

\section{C01. Models, metrics and distinct experiment
families}\label{c01}

\subsection{Deterministic network and metrics}\label{c01-deterministic}

There are \(N=6\) planar nodes. Let
\(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and

\[
f(x)=\alpha x+\omega Jx-b_{\mathrm{SL}}\|x\|_2^2x,
\qquad \alpha=b_{\mathrm{SL}}=1,\quad \omega=2.
\]

Let \(X=\operatorname{col}(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\),
\(F(X)=\operatorname{col}(f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_N))\), and let \(L\) be the
unit-weight undirected graph Laplacian. Define the arithmetic mean
\(y=N^{-1}\sum_i x_i\) and the stacked disagreement
\(\eta=X-\mathbf1\otimes y\). The network horizon is \(T=2\). Except
where a graph-specific gain is specified in C04, the coupling multiplier
is \(k_0=1.2\) and the graph is the six-node ring. The reduced reference
\(\bar y\) solves \(\dot{\bar y}=f(\bar y)\) from the arithmetic initial
mean. The snapshot is \(t_\star=T-10^{-5}=1.99999\).

The fiber-only deterministic model is

\[
\dot X=F(X)-k_0\kappa(t)(L\otimes I_2)X. \tag{E01}
\]

Reported disagreement is \(\|\eta\|_2\), not normalized by \(N\).
Projection-orbit error is \(e_y(t)=\|y(t)-\bar y(t)\|_2\). Phase error
is the continuously tracked angular difference of the mean relative to
the reduced reference, in radians. Coupling energy is
\(J_u=\int_0^{t_\star}\|-k_0\kappa(L\otimes I_2)X\|_2^2dt\). The
full-clock diagnostic requires additional physical input, accounted for
in C03. Time and state scales follow the numerical benchmark; no
physical-unit conversion is specified in the source records.

The finite-amplitude Cartesian array is defined, in node order, by

\[
x_i^0=r_i(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)^\top,
\] \[
r=(0.75,1.25,0.90,1.15,0.80,1.20),\qquad
\theta=(-0.75,-0.42,-0.15,0.18,0.48,0.82).
\]

The main time histories assign this state at \(t=0\). The graph-rate
tests assign the same array directly at \(t_0=1.6\). They do not use the
state obtained by advancing the main time history to \(1.6\). The
displacement state in C04, the unit-radius stochastic state below, and
the local-certified state in C08 are separate initializations.

\subsection{Nonlinear stochastic model}\label{c01-stochastic}

Let \(W=(W_1,\ldots,W_N)^\top\) consist of independent standard node
Brownian motions and let
\(e_\theta(t)=(-\sin\omega t,\cos\omega t)^\top\). The implemented
equation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
dX={}&[F(X)-k_0\kappa_K(t)(L\otimes I_2)X]dt\\
&-k_0\sigma\kappa_K(t)(L\otimes e_\theta(t))dW_t,
\qquad \kappa_K(t)=\min\{(T-t)^{-1},K\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{E02}
\]

The Laplacian acts on node measurement channels, not independent edge
channels. The reference tangent is common and deterministic, not a
tangent computed from each node or the current mean. Its time-dependent,
state-independent diffusion has zero Itô--Stratonovich correction. The
numerical rule is left-point Euler--Maruyama. Although
\(\mathbf1^\top L=0\), nonlinear drift can transmit disagreement effects
to the mean.

The nonlinear initial state has unit radii and centered phases

\[
\begin{gathered}
x_i(0)=(\cos\vartheta_i,\sin\vartheta_i)^\top,\qquad \vartheta_i=2(a_i-\bar a),\\
a=(0.08,-0.05,0.12,-0.10,0.03,-0.08)^\top,\qquad \bar a=N^{-1}\sum_i a_i.
\end{gathered}
\]

For a unit eigenvector \(v_j\) of \(L\), the scalar comparison starts
from \(\xi_{j0}=v_j^\top(\vartheta-\bar\vartheta\mathbf1)\). These are
phase offsets (arc lengths on the specified unit cycle), not Cartesian
sine projections. In co-rotating radial/tangent coordinates, the node
variational rates are \(-2\alpha\) and zero. Nonzero graph modes are
tangent to the node cycle and normal to synchronization. Their scalar
comparison is

\[
d\xi_j=-\rho_j\kappa_K(t)\xi_jdt-\rho_j\sigma\kappa_K(t)d\widetilde W_j(t),
\qquad \rho_j=k_0\lambda_j(L).
\]

Here \(\widetilde W_j\) is the orthogonally transformed Brownian
coordinate. This is a variational model, not an exact nonlinear
stochastic reduction. The nonlinear terminal cost is squared Euclidean
disagreement, \(\sum_i\|x_i(T)-y(T)\|_2^2\), rather than phase variance.
The scalar/modal comparator is evaluated with its declared phase-offset
initialization.

\section{C02. Schedules and complete deterministic
comparison}\label{c02}

The four fiber-only profiles are \(\kappa_0=1/T\),
\(\kappa_1=(T-t)^{-1}\), \(\kappa_2=T/(T-t)^2\) and the capped
inverse-time profile. All use initial coupling coefficient \(k_0/T\).
The cap is fixed at the stochastic comparator's
\(K_{\mathrm{opt}}=2.53979193583334\). The full-clock row instead uses
\(\dot X=\kappa_1[F(X)-k_0(L\otimes I_2)X]\) and accelerates the node
drift as well as coupling.

For interpreting the functional columns, define
\(E_\kappa(t)=\exp[-\rho\int_0^t\kappa(u)du]\),
\(I_\kappa^{(n)}=\int_0^T E_\kappa(t)^n dt\),
\(I_\kappa=I_\kappa^{(1)}\) and \(\rho=1.2\). These columns record the
integrals used in the comparison, not an empirical fit. The second-order
column corresponds to the node-average cancellation in the
Stuart--Landau model. Its mathematical justification remains in the
journal treatment.

The label ``Xu--Liu (\(\ell=2\))'' denotes the restricted single-weight,
symmetric regulatory coupling. Setting \(C(t)=(T-t)^2\) and
\(\bar k=k_0T\) gives coefficient \(\bar k/C(t)=k_0\kappa_2(t)\). This
row does not implement the complete directed, multiweighted controller
associated with that name.

\section{C03. Physical actuation and continuous-phase
diagnostics}\label{c03}

\subsection{Native-plant input and total energy}\label{c03-energy}

For the native plant \(\dot X=F(X)+u\), the full-clock realization
requires

\[
u_c=-k_0\kappa_1(L\otimes I_2)X,\qquad
u_w=(\kappa_1-1)F(X),\qquad u=u_c+u_w.
\]

With \(J_w=\int_0^{t_\star}\|u_w\|_2^2dt\), the physical energy is

\[
J_{\mathrm{total}}=J_u+J_w+J_{\mathrm{cross}},\qquad
J_{\mathrm{cross}}=2\int_0^{t_\star}u_c^\top u_wdt. \tag{E03}
\]

It is accumulated both by components and directly from
\(\|u_c+u_w\|_2^2\). Omitting the cross term is not the total-input
calculation. The normalization is exactly \(\kappa_1=(T-t)^{-1}\). In
the regular variable \(\tau=\log[T/(T-t)]\), the physical-time Jacobian
is \(dt/d\tau=Te^{-\tau}\).

The finite-amplitude DOP853 integration uses relative/absolute tolerance
pairs \((10^{-10},10^{-12})\), \((10^{-12},10^{-13})\) and
\((2.3\times10^{-14},10^{-15})\). The finest stored result is

\subsection{Common-deadline endpoint comparisons}\label{c05-transport}

\]

The raw \(\tau=25,30\) means differ by approximately
\(5.45\times10^{-11}\), which includes remaining reduced motion. The
smallest fitted network tail is approximately \(8.3\times10^{-12}\). The
reduced-flow round trip at one estimated point is

\(10^{-12}\) along the coordinate axes and diagonal in each run, record
sensitivity. These are consistency diagnostics, not random replications,
validated endpoint-error bounds or extra certified slope digits. The
original plotted curve is not replaced by a transported-endpoint curve.

\section{C07. Quadratic projection and nested refinement}\label{c07}

\subsection{Fixed-design covariance-aware vertex}\label{c07-vertex}

Use \(x=K/K_{\mathrm{opt}}-1\) on the seven ratios
\(\{0.975,0.990,0.995,1,1.005,1.010,1.025\}\). Let the design matrix
\(\mathsf X\) have rows \((1,x,x^2)\) and let \(Z_i\) collect each
path's seven outcomes. The pathwise coefficients are
\(b_i=(\mathsf X^\top\mathsf X)^{-1}\mathsf X^\top Z_i\); the
independent reference implementation evaluates the equivalent
least-squares problem by QR. Let \(\bar b=n^{-1}\sum_i b_i\).

The vertex is
\(\widehat K_{\mathrm{quad}}=K_{\mathrm{opt}}[1-\bar b_1/(2\bar b_2)]\).
Its delta-method standard error uses the sample covariance \(S_b\) of
the pathwise coefficient vectors, divided by \(n\), and gradient

\[
g_b=\left(0,-\frac{K_{\mathrm{opt}}}{2\bar b_2},
\frac{K_{\mathrm{opt}}\bar b_1}{2\bar b_2^2}\right)^\top,
\qquad \widehat{\operatorname{se}}(\widehat K_{\mathrm{quad}})
=\sqrt{g_b^\top(S_b/n)g_b}.
\]

Thus cross-cap covariance is retained. The reported values are

\[
\widehat K_{\mathrm{quad}}=2.5366693,\qquad
\widehat{\operatorname{se}}=0.00490771,\qquad
[2.5270498,2.5462888]\quad(95\%).
\]

The interval targets the seven-point quadratic projection of the
fixed-step mean curve. Its inclusion of \(K_{\mathrm{opt}}\) and
\(R^2=0.999728\) remove neither quadratic-model error, discretization
bias nor off-grid uncertainty. The archived reporting and independently
reconstructed last digits remain distinct numerical records.

\subsection{Pathwise extrapolation}\label{c07-richardson}

Form the covariance-preserving diagnostic

\[
Z_i^{\mathrm{R}}(K)=2Z_{i,h_{\mathrm{f}}}(K)-Z_{i,h_{\mathrm{c}}}(K),\qquad
\widehat M_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac1{n_{\mathrm{r}}}\sum_i Z_i^{\mathrm{R}},\quad n_{\mathrm{r}}=10000. \tag{E09}

If \(s_{\mathrm{f}}^2,s_{\mathrm{c}}^2\) and
\(s_{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{c}}}\) denote the sample variances and
covariance on those same paths, centering \(Z_i^{\mathrm{R}}\) gives
\(2(Z_{i,h_{\mathrm{f}}}-\widehat M_{h_{\mathrm{f}}})-(Z_{i,h_{\mathrm{c}}}-\widehat M_{h_{\mathrm{c}}})\).
Squaring and summing yields

\[
n_{\mathrm{r}}\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\,2}
=4s_{\mathrm{f}}^2+s_{\mathrm{c}}^2-4s_{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{c}}}. \tag{E10}
\]

The interval targets \(2M_{h_{\mathrm{f}}}-M_{h_{\mathrm{c}}}\). Removal
of first-order weak bias would additionally require an expansion
\(M_h=M_0+c_1h+O(h^2)\), which is not established by two step sizes.
